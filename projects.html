A Folk CRM and cold-email system to take the "Passport to Wellness" program to schools, principals and PTA/PTO groups, plus the training that lets a small team run it without me.

Folk CRMEmail sequencesGoogle WorkspaceDashboardsSOPs & training

The problem

Healthy Little Cooks had a strong reputation and a good program, but outreach to schools lived in spreadsheets and inboxes. Nobody could see who had been contacted, who replied, or who was ready for a call, and follow-ups depended on someone remembering.

What I built

  • A Folk workspace for 2,277 contacts with a 9-stage pipeline (Lead → To Contact → Contacted → Engaged → Call Scheduled → Follow-Up → Closed Won/Lost), a tag system (PTA/PTO, Title 1, Judy Center, Head Start, district and more) and saved views for each segment.
  • Four A/B email sequences for schools and PTO/PTA groups, 4 emails each, spaced 4–5 days apart. They send from a separate, warmed-up cold domain so the main domain stays safe. Stop-on-reply is on, and recipient filters keep existing clients and district offices out.
  • A live dashboard showing leads, pipeline stages, relationship type, sub-industry and partner types at a glance.
  • A booking tracker and reconciliation SOP: a twice-weekly routine to log meetings, move contacts and take them out of sequences.
  • A 35-page team guide with 30 annotated screenshots covering daily replies, the pipeline, adding contacts, running sequences and reading the numbers.

What changed

  • 7–8 meetings booked from the first wave of sequences.
  • School mail filters were auto-opening and auto-clicking every link, so opens and clicks were unreliable. I read the A/B test from replies instead. The "Family Engagement Programs" email won with a 14% reply rate, about 2.5× the other version.
  • The team now runs outreach, replies and booking reconciliation inside one system, with a guide to answer their questions.

Event venue CRM: from enquiry to venue tour to paid booking

Zoho CRM and Zoho Bookings set up so every tour request, booking and deposit across three halls moves through one pipeline, with automatic stage changes and payment reminders.

9+ workflow automations and 30+ email templates covering every step from tour booking to final payment.
★★★★★ "We went from juggling spreadsheets and phone notes to having a system that practically runs itself."Olaoluwa M. Oreniwa, CEO, NJS Royale
Zoho CRMZoho BookingsWorkflow rulesEmail templates

The problem

NJS Royale Event Centre was growing fast, but bookings ran on phone calls, spreadsheets and memory. Tours were scheduled by back-and-forth messages, payments were tracked by hand with no reminders, and nobody could see who had toured, who was ready to book or which deposits were outstanding. Follow-ups were missed and bookings slipped away.

What I built

  • Discovery first: mapped the full journey for the venue's three halls (Grande Royale, Signature and Ivycrest), including the rule that balances are due 14 days before the event.
  • An 8-stage Event Booking pipeline: Tour Scheduled → Tour Completed → Interested in Booking → Payment Pending → Booking Confirmed → Event Completed → Closed / Lost, with 30+ custom fields.
  • A branded tour-booking page in Zoho Bookings, with 30-minute slots, a shareable link and QR code. Every booking lands in the CRM automatically, and cancellations sync back.
  • Three payment plans: one-time, two-deposit and three-deposit. Layout rules show only the fields for the chosen plan, and field dependencies limit each plan to the payment stages that make sense.
  • 9+ workflow rules for lead-to-deal conversion, payment-stage updates, deposit tracking, due-date calculations, staff reminders and a fully-paid confirmation flow.
  • 30+ email templates for tour follow-ups, payment notices, deposit receipts, overdue reminders, booking confirmations and post-event thank-yous, all tested end to end.
  • Recorded video walkthroughs so the team could run the system themselves.

What changed

  • Every tour request is captured the moment it's booked. No more lost follow-ups.
  • Staff can see exactly where each client is, and deposits are tracked down to the last payment with automatic reminders.
  • Clients get professional, timely emails at every milestone, without anyone typing them.

The problem

CollisionCam captures crash leads and sends them on to partner attorneys and physical-therapy / medical clinics. Every lead lived in Zoho CRM, but the team couldn't see the big picture: how many leads came in this month, which counties they came from, which partner received them, and how many were signed or treated.

Every prospect follows the same path, from qualified → KYC → approved → first transaction → active client, with the next action set automatically.
Zoho CRMBlueprintWorkflow rulesPipelines

The problem

Cudium helps African SMEs and importers pay internationally. Their sales are relationship-driven (inbound, outbound and through agents), and they had just adopted Zoho with no structure in place. They wanted clarity and usability first, with only the automation that genuinely helps.

What I built

  • A leads board that shows contact progress at a glance: Not Contacted → Attempted to Contact → Contacted (interested / not interested).
  • Custom lead fields that match how Cudium sells: countries money is sent to, monthly transaction volume, business type, current payment methods and lead intent.

  • Recorded walkthrough videos so the team could use the system on day one.

What changed

  • The team works from one CRM where every prospect's stage, next step and history are visible.
  • Deals can't skip steps: KYC and the first transaction are confirmed before a client counts as active.

Octopus IntelligenceCompetitive intelligenceUK & UAE · 2026

Zoho One → Folk + Brevo + Make: a full CRM and email migration

Moved a UK/Dubai intelligence agency off Zoho One onto a lighter stack: Folk for relationships, Brevo for email, and Make keeping the two in sync. Now tagging a contact in Folk is all it takes to start the right emails.

Email lists reconciled 100% against the CRM and the sending domain fully authenticated. The one-off build became a monthly retainer.
★★★★★ "His expertise has been incredibly important to our development of our CRM and mailshot strategy."Octopus Intelligence (UK) · 5-star Fiverr review
Folk CRMBrevoMakePythonDNS / deliverability

The problem

The founder had years of data in Zoho One: around 2 million records, most of them stale or bouncing. He wanted a clean break. That meant a CRM he would actually use, weekly and monthly eshots and nurture automations that just run, and a place to log sales calls and see who needs chasing. All done for him, without the technical detail.

What I built

  • A careful migration. Only active, opted-in records (~20k) moved to Folk, cleaned, de-duplicated and tested on samples first. The old ~2M records were archived as an export and never mailed.
  • Folk set up for how he sells: six segments (leads, customers, former clients, inbound, spoken-to, information sources), a MENA / UK–US region split, a deal pipeline, and a "Needs chasing" follow-up view with reminders, notes and attached proposals.
  • Brevo on a dedicated sending subdomain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC all passing, plus a warm-up plan for the first sends.
  • Make sync in both directions: adding a contact to a Folk group adds them to the matching Brevo list. Unsubscribes and bounces in Brevo are written back to Folk so those contacts are never mailed again.
  • Automations and templates: the MENA nurture sequence that hands contacts to the weekly eshot at the end, plus weekly and monthly broadcast templates.
  • Python scripts to audit Folk contacts, screen new lead files before import, and check the sync and sending health.
  • A client setup guide so the founder could open the right Folk, Brevo and Make plans himself in 15 minutes.

What changed

  • Weekly (2,718) and monthly (1,276) email lists reconciled 100% against the live CRM.
  • The stack now runs at roughly $90–100/month instead of Zoho One. The true costs were shown up front, including the Folk and Brevo plan limits most setups miss.
  • The client chose to keep me on a monthly retainer to monitor, improve and grow the system.
